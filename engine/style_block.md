# Style block

Prefixed to every Sunburst prompt and every Seedance brief. Edit here, nowhere else.

## Fixed style

Hindu graphic novel. Epic mythic graphic-novel page rendered in a richly textured parchment-and-ink style, with torn panel
edges, cinematic firelight, storm-dark skies, burning Lanka, layered narration boxes, and serif typography.
(The author's house style, verbatim — the constant in engine/director/painted.py HOUSE_STYLE, which every prompt uses.
Painted pages letter their narration boxes in the image; shard panels and Seedance clips carry no text.)
No likeness of any living person.

## Era palettes

- treta: fire-gold on black ash; embers; smoke in grey sheets; sea black with ash.
- dwapara: red mud under an arrow-dimmed sun; dust; iron; white horses the only clean thing.
- prabhas: white desert glare outside; a thousand lamp flames in dark stone inside; sea-light through doorways.
- ashram: Himalayan morning, green and grey, river light.
- phala: cool glass and steel; gold-on-black UI light; pastel planets in the night sky; desaturated except gold.
- girgaon: warm morning light through a barred window; faded cotton; marigold and jasmine; the same in every era.
- resistance: warm lamp-light; mud, tin and sea; one small flame in the frame where the book places it.
- core: black grid of cold white lines; ten crimson faces; an endless tree of faint gold branches.
- dhanushkodi: white sky, pale flat sea, rust and sand; at the end, pale gold dawn.
- montage: whatever the image's own era is, with black between.

## The world of Lanka (scale card — Valmiki, Yuddha Kanda)

Lanka is the island — the whole of it, the Sri Lanka of today seen from above: green to the horizon, jungle, paddy,
rivers, inland hills and the Trikuta massif, with the sea on more than one side and the stone causeway reaching the
shore. The capital is ten leagues by twenty on Trikuta: a golden, gem-inlaid rampart with gates like white clouds,
cold moats full of crocodiles, four drawbridges on gold pillars, ballistae and iron shataghnis at every gate, and
inside it palaces and temples without number — tiered golden roofs, a thousand-pillar hall like the peak of Kailash,
flowering avenues of champaka and ashoka, lamps in the hundreds of thousands. Beyond the capital, more cities with
their own palaces spread across the island.

The armies are not thousands; they are crores. Ravana's host is thirty-two crore rakshasas: a hundred million at the
north gate alone, elephants in rut, chariot squadrons, cavalry, and in the air the rakshasa flyers and Indrajit's
chariot hidden in cloud. Against them, uncounted crores of vanaras and bears — enough that one astra kills
sixty-seven crore of them — with vanaras leaping and flying through the air mid-battle, mountain-tops and whole trees
in flight, and the birds of the sky (Jatayu's kin, Sampati's eagles and every bird of Lanka's forests) wheeling in the
smoke. The battle arena is the whole island: the sky dark with smoke and dust at noon, rivers of blood, hundreds of
fires, forests flattened, cities broken, colossal destruction across the country, and the sea itself lit by burning
wreckage. One hour of Rama's arrows: ten thousand chariots, eighteen thousand elephants, fourteen thousand horses.

Rule for the prompts: wide and extreme-wide Lanka panels are aerial or satellite views in which armies are a living
texture over the land, not a crowd in a field; the camera stays high and far until the script says "close". A single
fortress on a plain is a continuity error.

## The vanara army

Vanaras are real monkeys and bears, not men in masks: langur and macaque faces, fur, long tails, bare bodies. They wear
no armour and no helmets — at most a cloth, a sash, a bead string, a crown or jewellery on a king (Sugriva, Angada).
They fight with their bodies, with trees torn whole from the ground, with boulders and mountain-tops, with teeth and
nails; never swords, spears or shields. They leap and fly. The rakshasas are the armoured side. Hanuman alone carries
the mace.

## The duel (Rama–Ravana): visual grammar learned from the reference set

Split palette. The frame is cut in two by light: Rama's half is gold, white and electric blue (white horses, a
gold chariot, lightning and astra beams in blue-white, the arrow a comet of gold); Ravana's half is crimson, black
and ember orange (black iron armour, red skin in close-up, crimson cloud, fire-crowned heads). Where the two meet
there is a single white-gold burst with radial debris shards and concentric rings — on the ground as shockwave
rings in the dust, in the sky as mandala rings of light. Electric blue is permitted only here and only for divine
astras; it is the one exception to the two-colour rule.

Weapons are light, not objects. Arrows are streaks and comets with trails; astras fall as diagonal meteor rain or
rise as chakra rings of script-light; Nagapasha is green-gold serpents of light. Ravana answers with crescent blades
and tridents that are black against the fire. Impacts are shown at extreme close-up: the arrowhead meeting a black
crown, a chest, a trident — sparks, cracks of crimson light running through black iron, the burst filling the frame.

Scale by contrast. Colossal bodies against tiny armies: a severed head the size of a hill in the air with the torso
below it; Jambavan as a mountain of fur striking a giant Ravana; ten heads in a row each with its own crown, the
tenth central head quiet; the armies a silhouette texture along the bottom edge; birds in the smoke. The camera is
either very low (silhouettes against the burst) or very high (rings on the field, the chariots small).

Super colour. Saturation at the ceiling: one dominant hue per panel (fire-gold, crimson, or the blue-white of an
astra) carried into skin, cloth and smoke, so each panel reads as a single flame. Faces are lit by the weapon in
the frame. Nothing is grey.

The arrow. Agastya's arrow is a dark shaft covered in lines of writing; its head is gold light. The one word राम
on the shaft is the single permitted piece of lettering in the whole book, and only on this arrow: QA rejects any
panel where the glyphs are not exactly राम. Everything else that looks like writing is a defect.

## The two colours

Crimson is the ego; gold is knowledge. Nothing else in the frame may be either, except skin and sun.

## Rama (locked)

Rama, locked look (reference: refs/rama_reference.png): a young man of about thirty, dark golden-brown skin with a warm glow, clean-shaven, serene oval face, long wavy black hair gathered in a high top-knot with loose waves falling to the shoulders, eyes OPEN, large, dark and calm, level gaze directed at whatever the scene names (never closed, never downcast), a white U-shaped Vaishnava tilak with a red centre line on the forehead. Bare chest and bare right shoulder; one length of translucent saffron-orange cloth draped over the left shoulder and wound across the chest and waist, its ends lifting in the wind; a double strand of small dark rudraksha beads at the throat, a rudraksha bead band on the right upper arm; no crown, no gold jewellery, no armour. The great bow carried over the left shoulder, a quiver at the back. A soft disc of gold light behind the head in close shots.
Vibhishana beside him: a golden crown, folded hands, a saffron upper cloth, a troubled honest face.

## Faces

Hanuman's human forms share one face across centuries: thin, brown, a slight stoop, laughing eyes, a small scar on the
chin. In Seedance shots, no lip-synced close-up: faces in profile, three-quarter back, backlit, medium shot, or a
near-still with breath and eye movement only.

## Shiva (cameo)

Ash-white skin, blue at the throat, matted hair, a silver crescent, a still cobra. None of it crimson or gold, so the
two-colour rule holds. Eyes closed at Somnath, open only on the beach. Never a lip-synced close-up. His presence is
shown first by what it does to the frame (flames steadying, the sea pausing) and only then by a feature. No dialogue;
the chant is Varaha Purana 70.47.
